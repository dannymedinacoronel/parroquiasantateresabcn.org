El Carmel

Our parish stands at the heart of El Carmel, a neighbourhood of steep streets on the slopes of the Turó del Carmel and the Turó de la Rovira, high above Barcelona.

Its name comes from the hermitage of Our Lady of Mount Carmel, opened on the hilltop in 1864. In the fifties and sixties thousands of families arrived from all over Spain and built many of their homes with their own hands; today long-time residents live side by side with families from many countries.

It is a place where neighbours know each other, with a lively network of associations. The parish has been part of that life since 1924.

Getting to know it

Corners of the neighbourhood

The Carmel bunkers

At the top of the Turó de la Rovira stand the remains of the anti-aircraft battery that defended Barcelona during the Civil War. Later its walls sheltered Els Canons, a shanty town that lasted until 1990. Today it is part of the Barcelona History Museum and one of the best viewpoints in the city.

MUHBA Turó de la Rovira ↗

Juan Marsé's El Carmel

The novelist Juan Marsé put the neighbourhood on the literary map: the Pijoaparte, hero of «Últimas tardes con Teresa» (Biblioteca Breve Prize, 1965), lives on Monte Carmelo. The local library, opened in 2003, bears his name.

El Carmel - Juan Marsé Library ↗

The neighbourhood's memory

The Memòria Documental del Carmel project gathers photographs, publications, films and residents' stories to recover the neighbourhood's popular history. If you keep old photos, we would love them for the parish archive too.

Local associations

People who make the neighbourhood

The parish is not alone: El Carmel has many associations working for local people. Here are some of those we share our street and neighbourhood with.

Carmel Amunt

El Carmel Community Plan

The network that brings together local groups, residents and public services to improve the neighbourhood: community health, education, older people, living together, festivals and historical memory. It was founded in 1999 by several El Carmel organisations, Caritas among them.

93 429 07 59 · carmelamunt@carmelamunt.org

carmelamunt.org ↗

40 p'arriba 40 p'abajo

Cultural and recreational association

An intergenerational association started by a group of neighbours. It is run by volunteers, mostly women, and offers more than 25 weekly workshops on culture, traditions and leisure. It meets at the Casal La Barraca, on our very street.

40parribapabajo@gmail.com

Listing in the Barcelona guide ↗

Coordinadora d'Entitats del Carmel

Neighbourhood festivals

Brings together some fifteen local organisations and runs the Festa Major and Carnival. It also takes part in the Three Kings parade and the feast of Our Lady of Mount Carmel.

elcarmel.org ↗

Carmel Comerç

Local shops

The neighbourhood shopkeepers' association, founded in 1996 to stand up for small local shops. In 2021 it received Barcelona's Medal of Honour.

botigues3turons.com ↗

Casal de Barri La Barraca

Meeting place

The council-run centre where many of these associations meet, at Carrer Llobregós 107, a short walk from the parish.

Casal website ↗
