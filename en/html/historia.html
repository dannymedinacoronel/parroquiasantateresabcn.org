Our History

Home/History
El Carmel, Barcelona

Santa Teresa de Jesús Parish lies in the heart of El Carmel, Barcelona.

From the very beginning the parish was closely tied to the neighbourhood: it housed one of the area's first workers' cooperatives and a school for children and adults.

A century of history

Timeline

1923

The foundation stone

We have found photographs of the laying of the foundation stone of our beloved parish, dated 1923 (probably in May).

1924

Building the church

The church was built on the grounds of the old Can Grau estate (today the corner of Llobregós and Conca de Tremp) as a dependent chapel of Sant Joan d'Horta parish.

1927

A simple church and its bell tower

The works begun in 1923 are completed: a single-nave church and, beside it, the 17.82-metre bell tower that still shapes the neighbourhood's skyline.

Keep reading

The nave has a beamed ceiling resting on two round arches and ends in a semicircular apse. The whitewashed walls give the interior its characteristic white light. At the entrance are the vestibule and the choir loft.

In front of the church is an arcaded porch of three round arches —the central one taller— resting on columns over a low wall and closed with railings.

The bell tower, from the same period as the church, is simple and functional: an almost square plan, a moulded cornice, a hipped roof and an arched window on each side. Its rendered, cream-painted walls stand against the south wall, next to the porch.

According to the parish's entry in the inventory of Catalan bell towers (campaners.com).

1928

Church blessed

It was blessed in October 1928, as reported by La Vanguardia on the 20th of that month.

2005

The tunnel collapse

In February 2005 the parish was in the spotlight when it was affected by the unfortunate collapse of a shunting tunnel during metro construction work. The church courtyard is directly opposite, across the street from where the hole opened up.

Today

A century of life in El Carmel

A hundred years after it was built, the parish is still a meeting place for the neighbourhood: the Sunday Mass with gospel choir, catechesis for children and teenagers, Caritas, the youth and married couples' groups, and a community that remains open to everyone.
